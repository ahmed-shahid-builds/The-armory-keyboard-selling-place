Origin

WE DIDN'T SET OUT
TO BUILD KEYBOARDS.

We set out to stop replacing them. Everything after that was just figuring out how.

The short version

Obsidian Armory started as a frustration, not a plan. Every keyboard that came through the door eventually did the same thing — a switch would stick, a keycap would ghost, the backlight would start flickering right when it mattered. Nobody was building for the person who actually lives on the thing eight hours a day.

So we started tearing keyboards apart instead of buying new ones. Testing switches to failure on purpose. Rebuilding plates by hand until the flex disappeared. What came out the other side wasn't a hobby anymore — it was a standard we couldn't unsee, and couldn't find anywhere else on the market.

Four units later, that standard has a name. Red Igris leads it. The rest were built to earn their place beside it.
